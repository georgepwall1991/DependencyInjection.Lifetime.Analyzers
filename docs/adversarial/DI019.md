## Contract before implementation

### Shapes that MUST report

- A scoped service, an enumerable containing a scoped element, a keyed scoped service, or an activation graph reaching a scoped service when resolved through a proven root such as `app.Services`, `host.Services`, `IApplicationBuilder.ApplicationServices`, `IEndpointRouteBuilder.ServiceProvider`, a test host's `Services`, or `IServiceCollection.BuildServiceProvider()`.
- Root-provider aliases and direct/static framework resolution calls when the provider and service type are proven. Source-ordered straight-line aliases, `?? throw` guards, all-root conditional joins, and the documented always-evaluated conditional writes retain that proof.
- Singleton and hosted-service implementations resolving scoped services from an injected root `IServiceProvider`, including enumerable/keyed and EF Core registrations modeled by the collector.
- Transitive service graphs report the actionable resolution path, not merely the endpoint types.

Existing evidence includes `AppServicesResolvingScoped_ReportsDiagnostic`, `BuildServiceProviderResolvingAddDbContextServices_ReportsDiagnostics`, `RootKeyedResolutionWithScopedRegistration_ReportsDiagnostic`, `SingletonImplementationResolvingScopedFromInjectedProvider_ReportsDiagnostic`, and the transitive graph tests in `DI019_RootScopedResolutionAnalyzerTests`.

### Shapes that MUST stay quiet

- Resolutions through `IServiceScope.ServiceProvider`, `IAsyncServiceScope`, `HttpContext.RequestServices`, unknown providers, or providers whose root/scoped status is mixed or path-dependent.
- Same-named user-defined service-resolution methods/extensions, runtime service `Type` expressions, dynamic/unknown keys, and factory-registration lambdas already owned by DI003.
- Services proven singleton/transient, including known safe framework services (`IOptions<T>`, `IOptionsMonitor<T>`) and explicit registrations overriding ambient assumptions.
- Root-looking properties on arbitrary holder types: only known ASP.NET Core/Generic Host owners and exact framework extension symbols qualify.
- Field/property facts that depend on cross-method execution, and aliases written by deferred queries, branches, loops, unknown/ref/out/deconstruction writes, or goto paths, remain conservative.

Existing evidence includes `StaticGetRequiredServiceFromScopedProvider_NoDiagnostic`, `StaticGetRequiredServiceFromUnknownProvider_NoDiagnostic`, `ConditionalProviderAliasWithBranchDependentRootWrite_NoDiagnostic`, `UnrelatedServiceProviderPropertyResolvingScoped_NoDiagnostic`, and factory/user-extension negative tests.

### Deliberately unproven and therefore silent

- Arbitrary helper methods, properties, delegates, factory bodies, reflection, runtime `Type` values, custom provider conversions, and cross-file/cross-method provider state.
- Registration control flow that cannot prove one effective lifetime/key, and generic/keyed registrations whose overlap or key cannot be established.
- Automatic fixes for conditional-access/static-call shapes, scopes whose disposal/escape semantics are not proven, or any resolution whose receiver is not a normal member-access provider.

## Opposition review

1. **“Treat every `.Services` or `.ServiceProvider` property as root.”** A same-named property on an arbitrary holder may be a scoped/request provider; owner-type and exact framework-symbol checks are necessary.
2. **“Report every provider-like receiver in singleton code.”** A singleton method can receive or derive an unknown provider, and cross-method field/property writes cannot prove root lifetime. Keep the fallback narrow and preserve unknown silence.
3. **“Infer a service lifetime from registration name or a single branch.”** Conditional registrations and key mismatches are not a proof of effective resolution; stay silent rather than guessing.
4. **“Fix every diagnostic by inserting a scope.”** Conditional access, static calls, nested expressions, and disposal/escape-sensitive statements can make an automatic rewrite uncompilable or semantically wrong; refuse them.
5. **“Broaden resolution to same-named extensions.”** Binding to the exact Microsoft.Extensions.DependencyInjection extension symbol is the only high-confidence proof that a call resolves from the container.

## Resulting contract for this pass

Preserve DI019's narrow root/provider and lifetime proofs. Any change must close a demonstrated false positive or false negative with a focused regression test, without widening custom-provider or runtime-flow inference. The fixer must remain compilable and semantics-preserving on every offered shape.
